# Projectibility strength-language consistency review
This checkpoint proposes a coordinated change from potentially scalar-sounding language (`graded`, `degree of warrant`, `little projectibility`) to a consistent claim:

> Warrant for a projection can vary in strength without being reducible to a single numerical scale.

The revision keeps three points distinct:

1. For a fixed projective claim, warrant can be stronger or weaker.

2. Evidence relevant to that judgment can be heterogeneous and need not collapse into one score.

3. Support, defeat, and unresolved status remain relative to a declared claim and evidential standard.

## Proposed changes
### 1. Abstract definition
Current:

> I use projectibility for the degree of warrant for a bounded extension from observed to unobserved cases.

Proposed:

> I use projectibility for how strongly a bounded extension from observed to unobserved cases is warranted.
### 2. Formal qualification after Equation 1
Current:

> For graded warrant, Warr(C) means warrant sufficient for the declared use.

Proposed:

> Because warrant can vary in strength, Warr(C) means warrant sufficient for the declared use.
### 3. Introduction definition
Current:

> I use projectibility for the degree of warrant for one bounded extension from observed to unobserved cases.

Proposed:

> I use projectibility for how strongly one bounded extension from observed to unobserved cases is warranted.
### 4. Formal definition in Section 2.2
Current:

> Its projectibility is the degree of warrant for that extension relative to a declared target and use.

Proposed:

> Its projectibility is how strongly that extension is warranted relative to a declared target and use.
### 5. Entire second restriction
Current:

> Second, projectibility is graded but needn't be forced onto one numerical scale. An evaluator may report a predictive estimate and interval, a defeated invariance assumption, an unresolved population gap, and a supported narrow use. Collapsing those into a projectibility score would recreate the aggregation problem this paper diagnoses. The useful statuses are claim-specific: supported to a stated degree, defeated by specified evidence, or unresolved because the relevant evidence is absent or too imprecise. Each admits a qualifier naming its limit, such as supported narrowly for one request type, or conditional on evidence that only another party can supply.

Proposed:

> Second, warrant for a projection can vary in strength without being reducible to a single numerical scale. A report may therefore need to preserve heterogeneous results: a predictive estimate and interval, a defeated invariance assumption, an unresolved population gap, and a supported narrow use. Collapsing these into a projectibility score would recreate the aggregation problem this paper diagnoses. The summary status should remain claim-specific: supported under a stated evidential standard, defeated by specified evidence, or unresolved because relevant evidence is absent or too imprecise. Each status should name its limit, such as support for one request type or dependence on evidence that only another party can supply.
### 6. AGI application
Current:

> Even a commensurable, defensibly weighted index can have little projectibility to a new task, system, or period.

Proposed:

> Even a commensurable, defensibly weighted index may provide little warrant for a projection to a new task, system, or period.
### 7. Limitations
Current:

> Nor is projectibility a scalar property that can be calculated independently of a claim.

Proposed:

> Nor does variation in warrant make projectibility a scalar property that can be calculated independently of a claim.
### 8. Conclusion definition
Current:

> Projectibility names the degree of warrant for one bounded extension.

Proposed:

> Projectibility concerns how strongly one bounded extension is warranted.
### 9. Appendix wording
Current:

> Is the evidence independent to the degree claimed?

Proposed:

> Is the evidence as independent as claimed?
