### 1. What the typed-node account is claiming

Charitably read, the paper proposes a heterogeneous argument graph, not a world populated only by five-field empirical entities. An empirical node records the scope of an empirical claim—object, case population, conditions, outcome, and period—while an edge represents an inference whose assumptions and evidence connect one claim to another. Typing makes visible whether the conclusion delivered by one inference is genuinely the premise required by the next. The manuscript already recognizes that construct interpretation is different: it says a construct requires another template, invokes performances, contrasts, relations, bearers, manifestations, and stability conditions, and later distinguishes taxonomy, factor structure, capability interpretation, and criterion prediction. Figure 1 is therefore best understood as showing a possible construct-mediated argument, not as claiming that “capability” instantiates the empirical tuple. The problem is that the formal exposition never quite says this.

### 2. Answer: (d) a heterogeneous typed-claim graph with explicit bridge contracts

I recommend a fourth option: **(d) treat every node as a claim-content, allow different claim types, and type the bridges between them explicitly**. This combines the explicitness of (b) with the restraint of (a).

The first correction is ontological and logical: the construct itself is not the node. The node is an attribution proposition—roughly, “system \(S\) possesses capability \(C\), to degree or in form \(q\), over scope \(D\), under conditions \(K\).” Such a proposition can perfectly legitimately be the conclusion of an interpretation inference and the premise of a prediction or explanation. That is not a category error. The category error arises only if the abstract capacity is treated as though it were an empirical population or observable quantity passed intact from one study to the next.

The two arrows are then governed as follows:

- The incoming arrow is an **interpretation or measurement bridge**: specified performances, scores, contrasts, process evidence, and external relations warrant the capability attribution under some construct specification \(\Theta\).

- The outgoing arrow is a **manifestation or criterion bridge**: that same attribution bears a tested relation to target-task or workflow outcomes in specified bearers and conditions. If the bearer changes from a base model to an application or human–AI workflow, a further system-transport bridge is required.

- Their composition is governed by whether the attribution produced upstream is the attribution consumed downstream: the same construct specification, bearer or system population, degree or scale, manifestation and contrast commitments, scope conditions, period or version, and uncertainty. If those differ, the two arrows are not merely insufficiently warranted; their composition is not yet well-typed. An explicit mapping between the two attribution claims is required.

This is stronger than (a). Merely asking whether “a criterion relation” has been established is insufficient: one must know which construct attribution the criterion relation concerns and whether its bearer and scope match the upstream conclusion. It is less committal than (b), because the manuscript need not assert that every construct has the proposed five-part metaphysical structure. It can require a proponent-supplied **construct interface record** without pretending that the record constitutes a theory of construct meaning.

Option (c) is therefore only conditionally correct. A construct interpretation is certainly an attribution based on empirical performances, rather than another observation. But attributions are propositions, and propositions can be inferential way-stations. Removing the node altogether would obscure arguments in which a capability attribution genuinely functions as an intermediate premise. The paper should instead show that this path is optional: direct score–criterion or target-task evidence can bypass or replace it.

Realism changes the interpretation of the node, not whether it can be a node. A realist treats the attribution as asserting a genuine dispositional, functional, or causal property of the system; the outgoing relation may then be supported partly by an account of how that property produces its manifestations. An instrumentalist treats the attribution as an inferentially useful predicate or compression of conditional performance relations. It can still function as a premise, but if the direct empirical relation does all the predictive work, the construct node may be eliminable. Neither position licenses projection from the construct’s name alone: the realist must establish its manifestation conditions, and the instrumentalist must establish the predictive domain and stability of the profile.

The paper’s refusal to supply construct meaning is compatible with (d), but it imposes a limit. The paper may require the party advancing a construct claim to supply its interface record; it may not fill that record merely by writing “general reasoning” in a box. If no construct specification is supplied, the construct-mediated route must be marked unresolved or omitted. Neutrality about whether the capacity is real is permissible; neutrality about the content of the premise being passed between arrows is not.

### 3. Strongest objection

The strongest objection is that (d) may be (b) in disguise. Once the paper requires bearers, manifestations, contrasts, criterion relations, and stability conditions, it appears to have adopted a substantive, broadly dispositional or nomological conception of constructs. Different theories may individuate constructs through mechanisms, functional roles, measurement models, or operational equivalence classes instead. If each theory may supply its own schema, moreover, “type-checking” risks becoming metaphorical: almost any missing interface could be relocated into an unspecified external theory.

That objection is real. The answer is to call the construct record an **interface signature**, not a definition or sufficient theory of construct validity. It should contain only what the adjoining inferences actually consume. The substantive theory remains external, but the manuscript can insist that its relevant commitments be exposed.

### 4. Concrete manuscript changes

- In section 3.1, state explicitly that graph nodes represent **claims**, while models, populations, outcomes, and constructs are values or relata within those claims. The existing empirical tuple is closer to a scope or design record than a complete proposition; consider adding an asserted content \(\phi\), since incompatible empirical results could share all five existing fields.

- Rename the empirical type \(\mathcal N_E\). Introduce a construct-attribution signature schematically, without presenting it as a theory of constructs. It should expose at least the bearer scope, construct specification, manifestation and contrast commitments, boundary or stability conditions, criterion/process relations, temporal scope, and uncertainty.

- Define edges as inferences between claim-contents. If the target of \(e_1\) and source of \(e_2\) are not the same claim-content, require an explicit bridge \(m\); the relevant path is then \(e_2\circ m\circ e_1\).

- Revise the opening of section 3.2. Say that the empirical tuple directly locates mismatches at empirical–empirical interfaces, while construct interfaces instantiate analogous requirements through bearer continuity, construct-content identity, manifestation or criterion relations, stability scope, and uncertainty. “Outcome continuity” should explicitly include construct-to-criterion mappings.

- Redraw Figure 1. Replace “Capability or task claim” with two distinct possibilities: an optional, visually differentiated **capability-attribution claim**, and an empirical **target-task performance claim**. Include a direct predictive route from generalized benchmark performance to target-task performance, showing that construct interpretation may be omitted or replaced by direct target evidence.

- In section 6.2, say explicitly that its four roles are not an obligatory serial pipeline. A taxonomy, factor model, construct attribution, and external prediction may form different paths or provide convergent evidence. When the construct path is used, its exact attribution and criterion relation must be supplied; when the score is used only as a predictor, the graph may bypass construct interpretation.

- Qualify the reply to Freiesleben: construct meaning is not supplied by the projectibility audit, but it is a required external input whenever a construct-mediated path is asserted. Without that input, the path is unresolved—not automatically outside the audit’s concern.

### 5. Defect or reviewer’s itch?

This is a **genuine but bounded conceptual defect**. The central apparatus promises that typing makes interfaces inspectable, yet the most philosophically important node in Figure 1 lacks an explicit type, and “capability or task claim” conflates a theoretical attribution with an empirical performance claim. That matters to the paper’s own account of composition. It does not undermine the non-composition principle, the legal example, or the possibility of bypassing construct interpretation. The required repair is local. Demanding that the paper itself develop a full theory of capability constructs would be a reviewer’s itch; demanding that it state what a construct-mediated inference delivers and consumes is not.
