# Projectibility–warrant consistency checkpoint
The AGI paper should follow the distinctions already established in _Projectibility: A History and a Diagnostic Framework_:

- **Projectibility:** whether a bounded extension from observed to unobserved cases is warranted.

- **Support:** may be stronger or weaker for a fixed projective claim, without reducing to one numerical scale.

- **Scope and applicability:** fix the extension and determine whether adjacent links meet; they do not themselves warrant it.

- **Validity:** the broader judgment about an interpretation and use.

- **World-side reliability and grounds:** remain separate from warrant.

## Proposed manuscript changes
1. Replace every definition using “how strongly … is warranted” with:

  > Projectibility concerns whether a bounded extension from observed to unobserved cases is warranted.

2. Clarify the notation after the non-composition equation:

  > Here (\Warr(C)) means warrant sufficient for the declared use. This threshold notation does not imply that support for a fixed claim is binary or reducible to one scale; warranted links do not thereby warrant their composition.

3. Revise the second definitional restriction:

  > Second, projectibility is not a scalar property. Support for a fixed projection may be stronger or weaker, but support, scope, and the kinds of evidence bearing on the claim should not be collapsed onto one scale. A report should preserve heterogeneous results: a predictive estimate and interval, a defeated invariance assumption, an unresolved population gap, and a supported narrow use.

  The existing supported/defeated/unresolved explanation will remain, because those are claim-relative warrant statuses rather than numerical projectibility grades.

4. Replace “Projectibility names the standing of …” with a direct warrant formulation:

  > Projectibility concerns whether those parts of the argument that extend beyond cases observed under the source design are warranted.

5. Make the applicability distinction explicit in the interface section:

  > First, do the links meet? This is an endpoint-alignment question about whether the proposed composition is well formed.

  The next question remains whether warrant transmits across a well-formed join.

6. Rename the worked table caption from “Projectibility status” to “Warrant status” so that supported, defeated, and unresolved are not mistaken for scalar grades of projectibility.

7. Revise the limitation and conclusion so that only **support for a fixed projection**, not projectibility itself, varies in strength.

## Consistency guardrails
- Retain “stronger/weaker support,” “substantial/little warrant,” and “strength and kind of support” where they compare evidence for a fixed claim.

- Do not describe projectibility itself as graded, scalar, strong, weak, or varying in strength.

- Preserve the paper’s existing distinction between an undefined composition caused by endpoint mismatch and an aligned composition whose warrant fails to transmit.

- Preserve the narrower role of projectibility inside argument-based validity and the separation between empirical warrant and policy choice.
